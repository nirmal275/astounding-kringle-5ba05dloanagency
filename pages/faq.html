Personal Loan

Quick answers about unsecured personal loans

Most lenders require a minimum monthly salary of ₹15,000 for salaried employees. Self-employed individuals need a minimum annual income of ₹2 lakhs (ITR-declared). Easy Loan Services connects you with lenders who offer flexible eligibility criteria — some accept applicants earning ₹12,000/month in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities.
Higher salary typically unlocks lower interest rates and higher loan amounts.

With Easy Loan Services, personal loan approvals typically happen within 24–48 hours for eligible applicants with complete documents. Disbursement is processed within 1–3 working days after final approval. Pre-approved offers can be disbursed in as little as 2 hours.

Personal loan amounts range from ₹50,000 to ₹40 lakhs depending on your income, credit score, employment type, and repayment capacity. Salaried professionals in Tier-1 cities with good CIBIL scores can typically access higher loan amounts.

Yes! Self-employed individuals, business owners, freelancers, and professionals like doctors or CAs are eligible for personal loans. You will need to provide your ITR for the last 2 years, bank statements, and business proof. Lenders generally require a minimum business vintage of 2 years.
Maintaining a separate business bank account with healthy cash flow strengthens your application.

Missing an EMI has the following consequences:
  • Late payment penalty of 1–3% on the overdue amount
  • Negative impact on your CIBIL/credit score (reported after 30 days)
  • Repeated defaults may result in loan recall by the lender
  • Collection calls and legal action in severe cases
Contact your lender or Easy Loan Services immediately if you anticipate difficulty in paying — many lenders offer a brief moratorium or restructuring.

Yes, there is no legal restriction on holding multiple personal loans simultaneously. However, lenders assess your Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio — if existing EMIs exceed 40–50% of your monthly income, approval for a new loan may be difficult or come at a higher interest rate.

Personal loans are 100% unsecured — no collateral, security, or guarantor is required. The loan is sanctioned purely on the basis of your creditworthiness, income, and repayment history. This makes personal loans quick to process but slightly higher in interest rate compared to secured loans.

Personal loan interest rates in India typically range from 10.5% to 24% per annum, depending on your lender, credit score, income, and employment type. Applicants with CIBIL scores above 750 and stable income generally receive the best rates (10.5%–13%). Use our EMI Calculator to compare monthly payments at different rates.

Yes. Most lenders allow foreclosure after 6–12 EMIs have been paid. Foreclosure charges typically range from 0% to 5% of the outstanding principal. As per RBI guidelines, NBFCs and banks offering floating-rate personal loans cannot charge prepayment penalties. Always check your loan agreement for specific terms.
Foreclosing early reduces total interest outgo significantly — calculate savings with our EMI tool.

Home Loan

Guidance for your biggest financial investment

Home loans are available for tenures up to 30 years (360 months) with most banks and HFCs. Longer tenures reduce your monthly EMI but increase the total interest paid. The maximum tenure is also limited by the applicant's age — most lenders require the loan to be repaid by age 60–70.

As per RBI guidelines, lenders can finance up to 75–90% of the property's market value (Loan-to-Value ratio). The remaining 10–25% must be arranged as a down payment by the borrower. For properties above ₹75 lakhs, the maximum LTV is 75%, meaning a minimum 25% down payment is required.
A higher down payment reduces your loan burden and interest outgo substantially.

Yes, home loans are available for under-construction properties. The loan amount is disbursed in stages linked to construction progress. During construction, you typically pay only interest (pre-EMI) on the disbursed amount. Full EMI repayment begins once the property is ready for possession.

Yes! Home loans provide significant tax benefits:
  • Section 24(b): Deduction up to ₹2 lakhs per year on interest paid
  • Section 80C: Deduction up to ₹1.5 lakhs per year on principal repayment
  • Section 80EE / 80EEA: Additional ₹50,000 deduction for first-time home buyers (subject to conditions)
These benefits apply under the old tax regime. Consult your tax advisor for the latest applicable rules.

Yes, home loan balance transfer (BT) is a popular option to reduce your EMI burden. You can transfer your outstanding loan to another lender offering a lower interest rate. Easy Loan Services facilitates seamless balance transfers with minimal paperwork. Processing fees and legal charges may apply.
A rate difference of just 0.5% can save you lakhs over a 20-year loan tenure.

Fixed Rate: The interest rate remains constant throughout the loan tenure, ensuring predictable EMIs. Typically 1–2% higher than floating rates initially.

Floating Rate: Linked to the lender's external benchmark (RBI Repo Rate). Rates and EMIs may change periodically. Usually lower in the long run during a declining rate environment. Most home buyers in India prefer floating rates for long-tenure loans due to the lower overall cost.

Yes! A joint home loan (typically with a spouse or parent) combines both applicants' incomes, allowing you to qualify for a significantly higher loan amount. Both co-applicants share equal repayment responsibility and can individually claim tax benefits. Joint applicants must jointly be co-owners of the property.

In such cases, the legal heirs or co-applicants become responsible for loan repayment. Many lenders offer home loan protection insurance (credit life insurance) that settles the outstanding loan amount in case of the primary borrower's death, safeguarding the family's home. We strongly recommend taking this cover at loan sanction.

Home loan interest in India is calculated on a reducing balance (daily/monthly) basis. As you repay the principal each month, the interest charged reduces accordingly. This is more borrower-friendly than flat-rate calculations. The EMI formula is: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / [(1+r)ⁿ – 1], where P = principal, r = monthly rate, n = tenure in months.

Fuel your growth with the right business financing

Most lenders require a minimum business vintage of 2–3 years for unsecured business loans. For secured loans (LAP) or government-backed MUDRA loans, even newer businesses may qualify. Startups with strong financials or collateral can access tailored financing options we offer.

Yes! Under the CGTMSE (Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises) scheme, MSMEs can access collateral-free loans up to ₹2 crores. Additionally, many NBFCs offer unsecured business loans based solely on bank statements, GST returns, and ITR. Easy Loan Services specialises in finding the right scheme for your business.
Maintaining clean GST compliance and healthy bank turnover significantly improves your eligibility.

Unsecured business loans typically go up to ₹50 lakhs – ₹1 crore. Secured loans against property can go up to ₹10 crores or more, depending on the property value and business financials. The amount is assessed based on your turnover, profitability, credit history, and repayment capacity.

Business loan rates depend on several factors:

It is ideal for businesses with seasonal cash flow, exporters, wholesalers, manufacturers, and retailers. It is usually available as an overdraft (OD), cash credit (CC), or bill discounting facility.

Yes, several government-backed schemes support MSME financing:

  • CGTMSE: Collateral-free guarantees for loans up to ₹2 crores
  • Stand-Up India: ₹10 lakhs to ₹1 crore for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs
  • PM SVANidhi: Micro-credit for street vendors
  • PMEGP: Subsidy-linked loans for manufacturing & service units
Easy Loan Services guides you through the right scheme and application process.

A low personal CIBIL score makes unsecured business loan approval difficult. However, options include:
  • Secured loans against property or assets
  • MUDRA loans (less credit-score dependent)
  • Fintech NBFCs with alternative credit assessment
  • Adding a guarantor with a strong CIBIL score
  • P2P lending platforms
We recommend working on credit score improvement before applying — we offer a free credit advisory consultation.

Business loan tenures vary by type:
  • Working capital loans / OD: Revolving credit, renewed annually
  • Short-term business loans: 6 months to 3 years
  • Medium-term loans: 3 to 7 years
  • Secured / LAP loans: Up to 15 years
Longer tenures reduce monthly EMI but increase total interest cost. Choose based on your projected cash flows.

EMI & Interest

Understand your repayment and interest calculations

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is a fixed monthly payment made by a borrower to a lender. It consists of both principal repayment and interest. The formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / [(1+r)ⁿ – 1]

Where P = Loan Principal, r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100), n = Loan Tenure in Months. In early months, EMI is interest-heavy; as tenure progresses, principal component increases.
Use our free EMI Calculator to instantly get your exact monthly payment.

Yes, there are two ways to reduce EMI on a live loan:
  • Part-prepayment: Pay a lump sum to reduce the outstanding principal, after which you can request the lender to either reduce EMI or shorten the tenure
  • Balance Transfer: Move your loan to a lender offering a lower interest rate, which directly reduces your EMI
Most lenders are more willing to shorten tenure (less risk) than reduce EMI — request your preference explicitly.

Flat Rate: Interest is calculated on the original loan amount throughout the tenure. Example: ₹10L at 10% flat for 5 years = ₹5L total interest.

Reducing Balance: Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal after each repayment. Same loan at 10% reducing = approx. ₹2.75L total interest.

All home loans and most personal loans use reducing balance. Always ask lenders to quote reducing balance rates for fair comparison.

Financial advisors recommend keeping your total loan EMI obligations (including all existing loans) within 40–50% of your monthly take-home income. This is called the Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio. Most lenders also use this ratio during credit assessment. Keeping DTI below 40% ensures financial comfort and improves loan eligibility.

A moratorium is a temporary pause on EMI repayment granted by the lender. During the moratorium period, interest continues to accrue on the outstanding principal. At the end of the moratorium, borrowers either pay accumulated interest or have it added to the principal (increasing future EMIs or tenure). Moratoriums are typically offered during financial distress, natural disasters, or as part of loan restructuring.

For floating-rate home loans linked to EBLR (External Benchmark Lending Rate), a change in RBI's Repo Rate directly impacts your loan rate. When the Repo Rate increases by 0.25%, your home loan rate also increases by 0.25%, leading to higher EMIs or longer tenure. Conversely, rate cuts reduce your EMI burden. Fixed-rate loans are unaffected by repo rate changes.
Floating-rate borrowers benefit significantly when RBI enters a rate-easing cycle.

Processing fee is a one-time charge levied by the lender for evaluating and processing your loan application. It typically ranges from 0.5% to 3% of the loan amount (plus GST at 18%). It is usually deducted from the disbursed loan amount or paid upfront. Processing fees are not included in monthly EMIs — they are separate from the repayment schedule.

Some lenders allow EMI due date changes (e.g., from the 5th to the 15th of each month), especially if your salary credit date has changed. This facility is usually available once during the loan tenure. Contact your lender's customer service team or loan account manager. Easy Loan Services can also assist with this request for our partner lenders.

Making a part-prepayment (lump-sum payment toward principal) has these effects:
  • Reduces outstanding principal immediately
  • Total interest payable decreases significantly
  • You can choose to either reduce EMI or shorten tenure (shortening tenure saves more)
Example: On a ₹50L, 20-year home loan at 9%, a ₹5L prepayment in year 3 can save over ₹8L in total interest and cut 3+ years off tenure.
Making annual lump-sum prepayments (e.g., from bonus) is one of the best wealth-building strategies for homeowners.

Documents

Know exactly what paperwork you need

For salaried applicants:
  • Identity Proof: Aadhaar Card, PAN Card, Passport, Voter ID
  • Address Proof: Aadhaar, Utility Bill, Rental Agreement
  • Last 3 months' Salary Slips
  • Last 6 months' Bank Statements (salary account)
  • Form 16 / IT Returns for last 1–2 years
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Duly filled and signed application form
Keep digital copies (PDF) ready — many lenders now accept e-KYC and digital documents.

In addition to standard KYC and income documents, home loan applicants need:
  • Property sale agreement / allotment letter
  • Title deed and chain of title documents
  • NOC from builder / housing society
  • Approved building plan and layout
  • Occupancy Certificate (for ready-to-move properties)
  • Property tax receipts (last 3 years)
  • Encumbrance Certificate (EC) from Sub-Registrar

Business loan applications require:
  • KYC documents of all promoters/directors
  • Business registration proof (GST, MSME certificate, Shop Act)
  • Last 2 years' ITR with computation
  • Last 2 years' audited financial statements (P&L, Balance Sheet)
  • Last 12 months' business bank statements
  • GST returns for the last 12 months
  • Partnership deed / MOA-AOA / LLP agreement
  • For secured loans: Property documents / collateral details

Aadhaar is not mandatorily required for every loan. However, it is the most widely accepted document for identity and address proof and significantly speeds up KYC verification (via e-KYC / OTP). Most lenders strongly prefer it. If Aadhaar is not available, alternative identity proofs like Passport, Driving Licence, or Voter ID can be submitted.

Yes. If you don't have Form 16 (usually issued by employers for employees in the taxable income bracket), you can submit:
  • Last 6 months' salary slips
  • Income Tax Returns (ITR) for the last 1–2 years
  • Bank statements showing regular salary credit
Some lenders accept a combination of bank statements and salary slips as an alternative to Form 16.

For most loans, self-attested photocopies are sufficient for the initial application stage. However, originals are typically required for verification purposes at the time of document collection by the lender's representative. Originals are NOT permanently retained by the lender (except for property documents held as security for home/LAP loans until full repayment).

If your Aadhaar address doesn't match your current residence, you can supplement it with an additional address proof showing your current address — such as:
  • Latest utility bill (electricity, piped gas, telephone)
  • Rental/lease agreement (registered)
  • Bank account statement showing current address
  • Latest property tax receipt
Update your Aadhaar address at the nearest Aadhaar Seva Kendra for long-term convenience.

Yes! Most modern lenders and fintechs accept digitally signed documents, DigiLocker documents, e-KYC via Aadhaar OTP, and scanned PDF/JPEG copies. Banks regulated under RBI's digital lending guidelines accept e-NACH mandates, e-sign, and fully digital document submission. Easy Loan Services facilitates end-to-end digital applications with our lending partners.
Linking DigiLocker to your account gives instant access to all government-issued documents.

After full loan repayment, your original property documents (title deeds, sale agreement, etc.) held as security by the lender must be returned to you within 30 days of loan closure, as mandated by RBI guidelines (effective December 2023). The lender must also issue a NOC confirming full repayment and lien release. Failure to return documents within this period attracts a penalty of ₹5,000 per day of delay.

Eligibility

Find out if you qualify for a loan

  • 700–749: Good — most loans approved at competitive rates
  • 650–699: Fair — approval possible at higher rates with conditions
  • Below 650: Difficult — secured loan or guarantor may be needed
  • -1 or NH: No credit history — some lenders offer first-time borrower products

Age requirements vary by loan type:

Yes. Existing loan obligations are assessed via your FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio) — the ratio of all monthly loan EMIs to your gross income. Debt consolidation or closing small existing loans before applying can help.
