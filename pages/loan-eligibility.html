Key Factors

What Determines Your Loan Eligibility?

Lenders evaluate multiple parameters before approving a loan. Understanding these factors helps you prepare a stronger application and negotiate better terms.

CIBIL / Credit Score

Your CIBIL score is the most critical factor. Lenders use it to assess your creditworthiness and repayment history. A higher score means lower risk for the lender and better loan terms for you.

300 (Poor)750+ (Excellent)
Ideal: 750+ for best rates. 700–749 for moderate approval. Below 650 – high risk of rejection.
Monthly Income & Salary

Your take-home salary directly determines the maximum EMI you can afford. Lenders generally allow up to 50–60% of your net income towards total monthly EMI obligations.

Minimum for Personal Loan ₹15,000/mo
Minimum for Home Loan ₹25,000/mo
Minimum for Business Loan ₹30,000/mo
Employment Stability

Lenders prefer applicants with stable employment history. Salaried employees working with reputed organisations for 1+ years are favoured. Self-employed individuals need 2+ years of business continuity.

Government / PSU employees – highest preference
MNC / large private firm employees
Small business owners (2+ yr vintage)
Frequent job-changers or probation period
Age of the Applicant

Age influences the maximum loan tenure available to you. Younger applicants qualify for longer tenures, making loan repayment more manageable. Senior applicants may face restrictions closer to retirement age.

18–25 yrs Eligible but limited credit history
26–45 yrs Sweet spot – best eligibility & tenure
46–65 yrs Shorter tenure, higher scrutiny
FOIR – Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio

FOIR is the percentage of your monthly income already committed to existing loan EMIs. Lenders cap this at 40–50%. A lower FOIR means higher loan eligibility for new loans.

FOIR Formula
FOIR = (Existing EMIs ÷ Net Income) × 100
Lenders prefer FOIR below 40–50% for approval.
Existing Liabilities & Debt

The number and amount of outstanding loans and credit card dues directly affect your eligibility. Multiple active loans signal higher risk, reducing the amount you can borrow additionally.

  • Active home loan EMIs
  • Vehicle loan repayments
  • Credit card outstanding bills
  • Personal loan EMIs
  • Any other co-borrower obligations

Expert Tips

8 Proven Ways to Boost Your Eligibility

Not eligible for the full amount you need? Follow these expert-backed tips to strengthen your loan application and get approved faster at better rates.

1
📈
Improve Your CIBIL Score

Pay all existing EMIs and credit card bills on time, every time. Even one missed payment can drop your score by 50–100 points. Set auto-pay reminders to stay consistent.

2
💳
Reduce Credit Card Utilization

Keep your credit card usage below 30% of your total credit limit. High utilization signals financial stress to lenders and negatively impacts your CIBIL score significantly.

3
💰
Clear Existing Debts First

Pay off smaller loans and credit card balances before applying for a new loan. This reduces your FOIR and shows lenders that you have the capacity to take on new obligations.

4
👥
Add a Co-Applicant

Adding a spouse or parent as a co-applicant with a stable income significantly boosts your combined loan eligibility. It also improves approval chances if your individual profile has gaps.

5
💼
Declare All Income Sources

Include rental income, freelance earnings, dividends, and other legitimate income streams when applying. A higher declared income directly increases the loan amount you are eligible for.

6
🏦
Apply With Your Existing Bank

Approaching a bank where you already have a savings or salary account often leads to faster approvals and better rates. Your banking relationship serves as an implicit trust signal.

7
📋
Avoid Multiple Loan Inquiries

Every hard inquiry on your credit report from a lender temporarily reduces your score. Apply to only one or two lenders at a time. Use loan comparison platforms that do soft checks only.

8
📅
Opt for a Longer Tenure

A longer repayment period reduces your monthly EMI burden, improving your FOIR ratio. This makes you appear more financially comfortable to lenders and can increase your approved amount.

Most banks and NBFCs require a minimum CIBIL score of 700–750 for personal loans and 650–700 for home loans. A score above 750 gives you the best chance of approval at the lowest interest rates. Gold loans are an exception — they don't require a minimum credit score since they are secured by your gold assets. If your score is below 650, we recommend spending 6–12 months improving it before applying.

For salaried individuals, most lenders require a minimum net monthly income of ₹15,000–₹25,000 for personal loans. In metro cities like Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru, the threshold is often ₹25,000/month. Self-employed individuals typically need to show an annual net profit of at least ₹3–5 lakhs. Higher income generally means a higher eligible loan amount, as lenders calculate affordability based on 40–50% of net monthly income.

Lenders use the FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) method. They allow a maximum of 40–50% of your net monthly income towards total EMI obligations. The formula is:

Maximum eligible EMI = (Net Salary × 50%) − Existing EMIs

For example, if you earn ₹60,000/month and pay ₹10,000 in existing EMIs, your maximum eligible EMI = ₹30,000 − ₹10,000 = ₹20,000. Based on this EMI, lenders calculate the total loan amount you can borrow at the prevailing interest rate for the chosen tenure.

Yes, self-employed individuals — including freelancers, business owners, and professionals — can definitely get loans. However, the documentation requirements are more extensive. You'll typically need at least 2–3 years of ITR filings, bank statements for 12 months, profit & loss statements, and business continuity proof. A good CIBIL score and healthy business cash flows significantly improve approval chances. Secured loans like home loans and gold loans are generally easier to obtain for self-employed applicants.

A good CIBIL score is important but not the only factor. Common reasons for rejection even with a high score include:
  • High FOIR: Existing EMIs consuming more than 50% of your income
  • Unstable employment: Frequent job changes or short tenure at current employer
  • Too many recent inquiries: Applying to multiple lenders simultaneously
  • Incomplete or incorrect documents: Mismatch in details across documents
  • Employer category: Working for a company not on the lender's approved list
  • Age restrictions: Applicant too close to retirement age
Contact our experts to identify the specific reason and resolve it before reapplying.

The timeline depends on what needs improvement:
  • Clearing a small debt: Immediately improves FOIR — can reapply within 1–2 months
  • Improving CIBIL score from 650 to 720: Typically 6–12 months of consistent repayment behaviour
  • Employment stability: Most lenders require 1 year at current job; 6 more months of waiting may help
  • Adding income sources: Immediate if you can document additional income via ITR or bank statements
