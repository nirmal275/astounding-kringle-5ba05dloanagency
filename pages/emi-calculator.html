The Formula

How is EMI Calculated?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is calculated using the standard reducing-balance formula used by all RBI-regulated banks and NBFCs across India.

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)n] / [(1+r)n – 1]
P = Principal Loan Amount  |  r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)  |  n = Loan Tenure in Months
Principal (P)
The original loan amount borrowed from the lender.
Rate (r)
Monthly rate = Annual interest rate divided by 12 and 100.
Tenure (n)
Total number of monthly instalments (years × 12).
EMI
Fixed monthly payment covering principal + interest.
Worked Example

Loan Amount ₹10,00,000 P = 10,00,000

Note: In the first month, most of your EMI pays interest. Over time, the principal component increases — this is called a reducing balance loan.
Smart Strategies

6 Ways to Reduce Your EMI Burden

Smart borrowers use these proven strategies to lower their monthly outflow and save lakhs on total interest payable.

Increase Your Down Payment

A larger down payment reduces the principal amount. Even a 10% extra down payment can slash your EMI significantly and cut total interest by 15–25%.

Opt for Shorter Tenure Wisely

Though a shorter tenure means higher EMI, you pay much less total interest. Evaluate your monthly surplus and choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.

Negotiate a Lower Interest Rate

A good CIBIL score (750+) gives you negotiating power. Compare lenders, use balance transfer options, and don't hesitate to ask for rate reduction after 12 on-time payments.

Make Part-Prepayments

Whenever you receive a bonus or windfall, prepay a lump sum towards your principal. This reduces your outstanding balance, cutting future interest and tenure significantly.

Consider Loan Balance Transfer

If market rates have fallen since you took the loan, transfer your outstanding balance to a lender offering a lower rate. Even a 0.5% reduction saves you thousands over the remaining tenure.

Improve Your Credit Score

A CIBIL score above 750 unlocks the lowest interest rates. Pay all credit card dues on time, reduce existing debt, and avoid multiple loan applications within a short period.

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender on a specified date each month. EMIs are used to pay off both interest and principal every month so that over a specified tenure, the loan is fully paid off. In the initial months, the interest component is higher, while in the later months, more goes toward principal repayment — this is called a reducing-balance or amortizing loan.

This depends on your lender's policy and your preference. Most lenders give you two options when you make a partial prepayment: (1) Reduce your EMI amount while keeping the tenure the same, or (2) Reduce the tenure while keeping the EMI the same. Financial advisors generally recommend reducing tenure, as it saves more total interest over the life of the loan.

Missing an EMI payment has several consequences. First, lenders charge a late payment penalty (typically 1–2% per month on overdue amount). Second, missed payments are reported to credit bureaus (CIBIL), which can significantly lower your credit score. Multiple missed payments can lead to loan default, legal action, and asset seizure in the case of secured loans. It's best to inform your lender in advance if you foresee difficulty.

The industry thumb rule is the 40% rule — your total EMI obligations (across all loans) should not exceed 40–50% of your net take-home monthly income. Most banks use a FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio) of 40–55%. Keeping your EMI-to-income ratio below 40% gives you a comfortable financial buffer and makes you eligible for better loan terms.

In most cases, the EMI is fixed for the loan tenure. However, there are scenarios where it can change: (1) If you have a floating-rate loan, the EMI may change when RBI revises repo rates. (2) You can negotiate an EMI step-up/step-down plan with certain lenders. (3) Making a part-prepayment allows you to reset your EMI (downward) or reduce tenure. (4) Loan restructuring under financial hardship may allow EMI revision. Always check with your lender for their specific policies.
